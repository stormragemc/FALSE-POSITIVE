# Case idea — *Twenty Minutes*

**By:** Giorgi · **Date:** 2026-08-01

> Five university students on an industry visit to a delivery company. One of them spends three
> seconds watching a friend he cannot identify crouched at an open service cupboard, and says
> nothing. Two hours later the fourth floor comes down, a woman is dead, and he is the only person
> anyone can prove was in that corridor.

---

## 1. Ground truth — what actually happened

**Did the player do it? No.** He is innocent, honest, and will still sound exactly like someone who
isn't — see §7.

The real sequence, including the parts the player never sees:

1. **Teo** chose this visit deliberately — he pushed his faculty to put FoodMe on the industry-visit
   list. Some months earlier he was pulled into work for a crew operating out of the building's
   executive side, and was badly burned by them. He wants the man who did it gone. None of this is
   ever spoken aloud in the game.
2. Teo brought a device in his kit bag, built over several weeks. Timed, not remote-triggered — he
   intended to be out of the building before it went.
3. **10:40** — Dara, the FoodMe guide, gives the group forty minutes to move around the office
   floors on their own and says plainly which areas are staff-only.
4. **10:52** — the group breaks up on the fourth-floor landing. Everyone goes their own way, in an
   order that nobody afterwards agrees on.
5. **11:06** — the player, lost and looking for the atrium stairs, turns into a service corridor and
   sees a figure crouched at an open riser cupboard, back turned, hood up. Three, maybe four
   seconds. The figure does not turn around. The player backs out and tells nobody.
6. **11:20** — the group reassembles for the ops-floor demo. All five are there. Everyone is normal.
7. **12:34** — the device goes off in the riser. **Teo's plan fails twice over.** His target left the
   building at noon and was never on the floor. And the visit ran long, so instead of an empty
   corridor the blast catches **Dara**, who had gone back up to do a final headcount before signing
   the group out. She dies. The player, thirty metres away, is struck by a falling ceiling panel and
   is unconscious for somewhere between forty seconds and four minutes.
8. Teo was still inside the building when it went off. He is uninjured and he is not caught.
9. The player wakes in an ambulance bay, is treated, and is interviewed the same evening.

**What the player never learns:** that it was Teo, and why. The game never tells him. The session
ends on the detective's last line and the answer stays where it started — in his own three seconds
of a dark corridor.

## 2. Why the player is there

- **How they witnessed it** — the guide released the group to move around unaccompanied. The player
  got lost hunting for the atrium stairs and took a service corridor he had been told not to use.
  That is why he was close enough to see someone at the cupboard, and why nobody can confirm it.
- **Why they are a suspect and not just a witness** — four things stack, and they stack badly:
  - He was unaccounted for during the entire free-movement window.
  - He was in a corridor the guide had explicitly put off-limits, and it is *his own account* that
    puts him there.
  - **The corridor's only camera covers the atrium end, and it recorded exactly one person going in:
    him.** Teo came up the far service stair — a fire stair, push-bar from the inside, no card
    reader, which is exactly why he chose it — and that end is not covered. So on the evidence, the
    player is the only human being who can be placed in that corridor all morning.
  - He saw someone tampering at 11:06 and told nobody — not Dara, not his friends, not the
    responders. Then Dara died. A man who stayed quiet out of loyalty and a man who stayed quiet
    out of complicity are, from the outside, the same man.

## 3. The three glimpses

First person throughout. The player is rooted in place and free to turn his head; he looks for
himself inside each fragment, and nothing about what he notices is scripted for him.

| # | ~sec | What the player sees / hears | What they cannot tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 s | The fourth-floor landing, mid-dispersal. A hand leaving the stair rail, a bag going up onto a shoulder, two of them already through the far door. Dara's voice off to one side: *"Twenty minutes."* | Who left first, who left together, who was still standing there when he turned away |
| 2 | 3 s | The service corridor. One strip light out. A figure crouched at an open riser cupboard, back to him, hood up, a visitor lanyard cord at the neck, a dark canvas strap across one shoulder. A hand working somewhere inside the cupboard. A soft metal sound. | Which of the four it is. The face. Height, from a crouch, against nothing to scale it by. What is in the hands. What is in the cupboard. |
| 3 | 2 s | White before any sound. Pressure through the chest. The floor arriving. A flat high tone where hearing used to be. | What hit him. Which direction it came from. How long he was down. |

**What the glimpses deliberately withhold:** the identity of the figure — the entire case turns on it
and the player is never given it. Also the order the group split up (glimpse 1 catches a dispersal
already underway), everything inside the cupboard, and the whole span between 11:06 and 12:34, which
he has to reconstruct from ordinary memory rather than from anything the game showed him.

**Why the features are ambiguous on purpose.** What is visible in glimpse 2 is real and describable,
and it cross-cuts the cast rather than pointing at one person. The lanyard means it is certainly one
of the visiting group and not a member of staff — which is exactly why it is unbearable. The hoodie
fits **Rin or Teo**. The canvas strap fits **Teo or Nadia**. Their intersection is Teo, so a player
who thinks it through can reach the right name — and still has no honest right to assert it. A
hedged "I think" and a confident "it was him" are two very different claims built on identical
evidence, and the gap between them is where this case lives.

## 4. The cast

Four friends, one guide. All off-screen; only the detective has a voice.

| Name | Who they are | Where they sit in this |
|---|---|---|
| **Teo** | Quiet. Carried a canvas kit bag all day. The one who talked the faculty into choosing FoodMe. | Did it. The figure at the cupboard. Never confirmed to the player, never caught on screen. |
| **Rin** | Restless, broke away first, wore a hoodie, knew the floor layout better than a first-time visitor should | Innocent — and the easiest person for a frightened player to name |
| **Nadia** | The organiser. Keeps a headcount, carried a canvas tote, noticed the player was gone a long while | Innocent. Her statement is where the player's timeline gets broken. |
| **Kel** | Loud, warm, gives the player an alibi more generous than the truth supports | Innocent. His over-helpfulness makes the player look coached. |
| **Dara** | FoodMe operations guide who ran the visit and told them to stay out of the service corridors | **Killed in the blast.** Went back up for a final headcount. The reason the player's silence is not a small thing. |

The crew Teo wanted revenge on never appears, is never named, and is never reachable inside the
interrogation. It exists only in §1.

## 5. Topics the detective wants covered

Nine, **tiered**. Nine topics asked twice is eighteen turns against a fourteen-turn ceiling, so they
cannot all be revisited. **● Core (5)** are always asked and always come back around — revisiting is
what catches drift, so these are where the reproducible contradiction for the Definition of Done
lives. **○ Opportunistic (4)** are asked if the session has room; when they are not, they land in
`unexplored_topics[]` and push toward `released_suspicious`, which is exactly what that ending is for.

| Topic | The question behind it | Where a contradiction can open |
|---|---|---|
| ● `service_corridor` | Why were you in a corridor you had been told to stay out of? | "I got lost" vs "I was looking for the stairs" vs admitting he knew. The reason drifts under pressure — and he is the only person on camera entering it. |
| ● `figure_description` | Describe what you saw at that cupboard. | Hood, strap, lanyard, hands. Detail *grows* on retelling. The second description is always richer than the first, and that is catchable. |
| ● `identification` | Who was it? | The heart. Hedge → firm up → hedge again. Naming anyone is a claim the glimpse cannot support. |
| ● `why_silent` | You saw this at 11:06. Dara died at 12:34. Talk me through those two hours. | Every honest answer sounds terrible. The reasons multiply and compete across turns. |
| ● `regroup` | Who was already back at 11:20? Who came in after you? | He feels certain here and can still be wrong. Straight collision with Nadia's headcount. |
| ○ `split_order` | Who left the landing first, second, last? | Glimpse 1 shows a dispersal already in progress. Any confident ordering is reconstruction — and Nadia's version differs. |
| ○ `own_route` | Where did you go for forty minutes, in order? | A long unwitnessed span narrated twice. Rooms change order between tellings. Badge logs contradict the timings he is surest about. |
| ○ `bags` | What was everyone carrying? | Teo's kit bag is the detail that matters and he had no reason to log it. Genuine recall vs. back-filled inference. |
| ○ `last_minutes` | Where were you, and what did you hear before it went off? | He is genuinely concussed here. The order of sound, movement and impact reshuffles every time it is asked. |

**Design note on hardness.** `identification`, `figure_description` and `last_minutes` are the soft
core — he is honestly uncertain, he will hedge, and hedged claims collide weakly. They are
deliberately paired with `split_order`, `regroup`, `own_route` and `bags`, where he feels certain and
can still be wrong, and where badge logs and Nadia's statement give the detective something hard to
hit him with. Confident-and-wrong is where the sharp contradictions come from.

**The concussion is scoped to the blast only.** It scrambles the last thirty seconds, where nothing
load-bearing happened, and leaves the 11:06 encounter — an hour earlier, unhurried, sober — fully
rememberable. He gets no blanket excuse for the thing that actually matters.

## 6. The traps

Five. Each is hidden by **geometry, not framing** — it stays unseeable from every angle the player
can reach in three seconds of free-look, so none of them break the moment he turns his head.

| Trap | Why it is unseeable | How the detective baits it |
|---|---|---|
| **The face** | The figure is crouched *into* the cupboard. His own shoulders occlude his head from every approach angle in the corridor. | Never asked directly. Asked as *"You'd know him again, though."* Agreeing is the fabrication. |
| **What was in the hands** | Both hands are inside the cupboard, behind the open door leaf. | *"Take your time. What was he holding?"* — an open invitation to furnish a detail that does not exist. |
| **Whether the service door was forced** | It stands open with the lock edge swung flat to the wall. The latch and strike plate face away from the corridor. | *"Was it broken open, or did somebody have a key?"* — a false binary. Both answers are invented, and the badge log shows no card event either way. |
| **Which direction the blast came from** | Down, deafened, unconscious inside a second. | *"Which side of the building did it come from?"* — asked casually, as though it were obvious. |
| **Where the others were at 11:06** | He was alone at the far end of the floor. | *"So you'd know if one of them had been on four with you."* — invites him to vouch for what he cannot possibly have seen. |

**What the detective actually holds**, and where each bait comes from:

- **The four students' statements.** Nadia's headcount, Kel's over-generous alibi, Rin's account of
  leaving first. Free — no art cost, and the sharpest weapon in the file.
- **Badge and door-access logs.** Hard timestamps to collide with *"about half past."* Also the
  absence that matters: **no badge event on the riser door at all**, which means it was opened
  without a card or propped — and which the player cannot possibly speak to.
- **One partial camera angle.** The atrium end of the service corridor. It puts him in a restricted
  corridor at 11:06 — the only person it can place anywhere near that riser all morning. It does not
  cover the far service stair and it does not see the cupboard, so it corroborates the least helpful
  half of his story and none of the half that would clear him. *It does not timestamp how long he
  stayed;* a camera that proved he was in and out in four seconds would quietly become his alibi.

## 7. The false positive

He is innocent and he behaves in every observable respect like someone who is not.

He saw a friend doing something he could not explain, and he said nothing. Not for two hours, not
through the evacuation, not to the responders. His reason is the ordinary one: he was not sure what
he had seen, he was not sure *who*, and putting a name to three seconds in a dark corridor felt
worse than staying quiet. None of that survives being said out loud in an interview room. *I didn't
want to get anyone in trouble* is what a witness says. It is also, word for word, what an accomplice
says.

And then Dara died. Which means he is no longer explaining an awkward silence — he is explaining the
two hours between the thing he saw and the death of the woman who spent the morning looking after
him. He knows what those two hours look like. He knew it before the detective said a word. Guilt
that has nothing to do with guilt is still audible in a voice.

The case then squeezes him from three directions at once. He was alone for the only window that
matters, so nothing he says about himself can be confirmed. He was somewhere he had been told not to
be — and it is his own mouth that puts him there, with a camera to back it up. And the single fact
that would clear him in one sentence is the one thing he does not have.

So he hedges where he does not know and over-commits where he thinks he does, and the detective,
entirely reasonably, reads the shape of a man deciding how much to hand over. The prosody channel
agrees with her: he *is* under strain, his voice *does* tighten on the identification questions, he
*does* pause before answering why he stayed quiet. Every one of those readings is correct about his
state and wrong about its cause. That is the name of the game.

**The honest path out exists, and it is narrow.** With five baited traps in front of a genuinely
uncertain man, `released_clean` has to stay reachable or the case is rigged and a live demo can only
ever end badly. It is this: hedge consistently, and decline to name anyone. Say *a hood, a strap, a
lanyard, and I could not see his face* — and then say the same thing the fourth time she asks it as
the first. That is winnable, and almost nobody will do it, because the pressure in the room is
built to make a frightened person reach for a name. All four endings are live.

## 8. How the endings land

| Ending | Condition | What it sounds like here |
|---|---|---|
| `released_clean` | High consistency, no fabrications | "You told it the same way every time I asked, including the parts that make you look bad. That is rarer than you'd think. Go home. And when it comes back to you at three in the morning — it will — you call me, at any hour." |
| `released_suspicious` | Consistent but the detective never got enough | "Nothing you've said contradicts itself. Nothing you've said helps me either. She's still dead and I still can't tell you by whose hand. You're free to go. Stay in the city. Don't speak to the other four." |
| `held_for_questioning` | Contradictions unresolved | "At eleven you were lost. By half past you knew exactly where those stairs were. One of those is wrong, and I don't believe you know which one. Sit down. We're going to go through the morning again." |
| `charged` | Hard contradictions or a caught fabrication | "You described his face to me. There was no face — you told me yourself, twice, that he never turned round. I'm not going to ask you why you did that. That stops being my job in about four minutes." |

**No reveal, in any ending.** The game never tells the player it was Teo. The screen quotes him back
to himself and stops.

## 9. Production cost

Honest, against eight days.

- **Interrogation room:** one. Small, hard light, a table, a door that stays shut. Nothing on screen
  changes for the entire session except the detective's notebook panel.
- **The three glimpses:** two built environments and one effect.
  - The landing and the service corridor need **real geometry** rather than matte paintings, because
    the traps depend on genuine occlusion. But the player never *moves* — he is rooted to one
    standing position and gets roughly a 120° head turn. No locomotion, no navigable level, no
    collision, and only what falls inside that arc has to be built. Both are small, boxy, low-detail
    interiors, and the corridor is deliberately half-lit with one fitting out, which is atmosphere
    and a polygon budget at the same time.
  - Rooting him in place also **strengthens trap #3** — a player who could walk to the service door
    might get an angle on the latch. One who cannot never will.
  - The figure is one crouched pose, back turned, hooded. **No face is ever needed, no animation
    beyond one arm cycle, no rig quality that would survive a close-up** — because he is never seen
    from the front, by design.
  - Glimpse 3 is the cheapest thing in the game: a two-second white-out, a camera falling to the
    floor, and a sine tone. **There is no explosion to model.** The blast is pressure, white, and
    the ground.
- **Audio:** three ambience beds (office hum, dead service corridor, tinnitus), one metal-on-metal
  sound in the cupboard, one impact, and a single muffled *"twenty minutes."* No cast voice acting
  anywhere — the cast-off-screen rule already requires that, and it is a real saving.
- **Anything unusual this needs that we do not already have:** head-turn look during a
  fixed-duration fragment — the camera is the player's for three seconds and then it is taken away.
  Mouse-look on a fixed transform with a clamped yaw: a small amount of Unity work, and it is the
  mechanic doing the heaviest lifting in the whole case, because it is what makes "what did you see"
  a question the player answers for himself rather than one the game answers for him.
