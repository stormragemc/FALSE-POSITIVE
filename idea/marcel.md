# Case idea — *FALSE POSITIVE: DEAD AIR*

**By:** Marcel · **Date:** 1 August 2026

> The player is the only witness to the murder of Dr. Elias Voss, the disgraced scientist accused of helping a company manufacture synthetic audio evidence. They are interrogated by an AI reconstruction of Voss himself—and must convince the dead man they did not kill him without revealing the evidence they came to steal.

## 1. Ground truth — what actually happened

Dr. Elias Voss co-founded **Sovereign Acoustics**, a forensic-audio company whose software was sold to courts, police forces and private investigators as a way to authenticate recordings. Internally, however, the company discovered that the same models could be reversed: if they knew what their detector considered “real,” they could generate synthetic recordings designed to pass it.

Sovereign was accused of manufacturing audio evidence, but every investigation collapsed. The incriminating recordings passed Sovereign’s own authentication system, the source files disappeared, and the only engineer willing to testify—**Nadia Reyes**, the player’s closest friend—was discredited by a synthetic recording that made it sound as though she had admitted to falsifying her allegations. Her career and mental health were destroyed. The player blamed Voss, the public face of the company, and repeatedly threatened to expose him.

In the weeks before his death, Voss discovered that Sovereign’s executives had continued the programme without him and had used his research to frame Nadia. He prepared an encrypted audit containing model logs, altered recordings and the identities of other victims. He arranged a secret meeting with the player in **Archive Studio B**, intending to give them the audit and ask them to deliver it to a journalist.

The player did not trust him. They entered through a maintenance corridor using Nadia’s old access card and carried an empty drive, intending to steal the files if Voss refused to surrender them. Their last message to him read: **“If you bury this again, I will destroy everything you left behind.”**

Before the meeting, Sovereign’s operations director **Mara Vale** confronted Voss. She triggered a ninety-second systems blackout, demanded the audit key and killed him with a heavy microphone counterweight when he refused. She then played a synthetic clip of the player saying, *“I came here to finish this,”* through the studio monitor, knowing the player was approaching outside. She escaped through the sound-lock with the master audit drive but dropped a damaged backup shard.

The player entered seconds later, heard the synthetic voice, saw Voss dying and instinctively pulled the backup shard from the console. They touched his wrist to check for a pulse, heard security approaching and hid the shard inside their jacket. They are found beside the body with Voss’s blood on their hand, an illegal access card, a history of threats and no honest explanation for why they came.

**The player did not kill Voss.** They are innocent of murder, but guilty of trespass, attempted theft and concealing crucial evidence to protect Nadia.

The interrogation is conducted by **VOSS-Continuance**, a posthumous AI reconstructed from Voss’s voice, journals, interviews, messages and forensic casework. The reconstruction ends three weeks before his death. It remembers the player’s hatred and threats, but not Voss’s decision to expose Sovereign, his arrangement to meet the player or his final admission that Nadia had been framed.

Worse, Sovereign supplied the reconstruction with a curated case file that assumes the player is the killer. The same company accused of manufacturing evidence has rebuilt its dead founder as the detective investigating his own murder.

## 2. Why the player is there

- **How they witnessed it:** The player came to Archive Studio B after receiving a one-line message from Voss: *“I can prove what they did to Nadia. Come alone.”* Through the maintenance-door window and then the opening door, they caught only three chaotic fragments of the murder’s aftermath.
- **Why they are a suspect:** They entered illegally with Nadia’s revoked card, had publicly threatened Voss, carried a drive intended for stolen files, touched the body and concealed the damaged audit shard. A security microphone also appears to capture the player saying, *“I came here to finish this,”* although the clip was synthetically generated.
- **Why they cannot simply tell the truth:** Revealing Nadia’s involvement risks exposing her location, reopening the fabricated case against her and giving Sovereign time to destroy the remaining evidence. The player must prove innocence while deciding how much of Nadia’s story—and Voss’s crimes—to reveal to Voss’s own digital ghost.

## 3. The three glimpses

This is the entire crime cinematic: three fragments, approximately eight seconds total, never replayed.

| # | ~sec | What the player sees / hears | What they cannot tell |
| - | ---- | ---------------------------- | --------------------- |
| 1 | 3 s | Through wired glass under pulsing red emergency light: Voss arguing with a tall silhouette beside the mixing console. The monitor plays the player’s own voice saying, *“I came here to finish this.”* | Whether the sentence is live, recorded or synthetic; the silhouette’s face, clothes and sex; what Voss is holding. |
| 2 | 3 s | The lights die. There is a dull metallic impact, a burst of feedback and Voss gasping, *“Not the voice—the logs.”* A door seal hisses on the far side of the studio. | Who struck him; whether the hiss is the sound-lock opening or closing; whether “the logs” refers to audio, access records or model logs. |
| 3 | 2 s | Emergency light returns. Voss is on the floor reaching toward a blinking console. A dark figure slips behind the acoustic partition. The player enters and grabs the object beneath Voss’s hand. | Whether the figure actually exited or is a shadow; what object Voss was reaching for; whether the player arrived before or after the fatal blow. |

**What the glimpses deliberately withhold:** Mara’s identity; the weapon; the colour of her clothing; the exact order of the impact and door hiss; whether the incriminating voice came from a person; what was taken from the console; and whether Voss summoned the player as an ally or expected an attacker.

## 4. The cast

All characters except the AI detective remain off-screen during interrogation.

| Name | Who they are | Where they sit in this |
| ---- | ------------ | ---------------------- |
| **Dr. Elias Voss / VOSS-Continuance** | Dead co-founder and chief forensic scientist of Sovereign Acoustics; now the AI detective. | Victim, compromised investigator and incomplete copy of a man who may have tried to undo his crimes too late. |
| **The player** | Nadia’s closest friend and the only eyewitness. | Innocent of murder, but present illegally, emotionally compromised and concealing the backup audit shard. |
| **Nadia Reyes** | Former Sovereign engineer whose whistleblowing was destroyed by a synthetic confession. | The person the player is protecting and the strongest explanation for both their fear and their motive against Voss. |
| **Mara Vale** | Sovereign’s operations director and keeper of the company’s evidentiary contracts. | The real killer; she murdered Voss to recover the audit and then seeded the reconstruction with a case designed to implicate the player. |
| **Jon Bell** | Sovereign night-security supervisor. | Found the player over the body; his access log is reliable, but the studio audio attached to it is not. |

## 5. Topics the detective wants covered

| Topic | The question behind it | Where a contradiction can open |
| ----- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `reason_for_arrival` | Why did the player come to the studio? | “To speak to Voss” conflicts with the empty drive, revoked card or a later admission that they came for files. |
| `relationship_with_voss` | Did the player hate or trust the victim? | Denying hostility conflicts with the threatening message; admitting hatred creates apparent motive. |
| `nadia_connection` | Who owns the revoked access card? | Calling it the player’s card conflicts with its identity record; naming Nadia exposes the secret they are protecting. |
| `heard_sentence` | Who said “I came here to finish this”? | The player may first assume the silhouette spoke, then realise it was their own voice from the monitor. |
| `silhouette` | What can the player identify about the second person? | Emergency lighting and wired glass make confident claims about face, clothing or sex unsupported. |
| `sequence_of_sounds` | Did the impact occur before or after the door hiss? | The blackout, feedback and panic make the order easy to reverse under repeated questioning. |
| `contact_with_voss` | Why is Voss’s blood on the player? | “I never touched him” conflicts with the physical trace; admitting contact may sound like concealment. |
| `object_taken` | What did the player remove from beneath Voss’s hand? | Denial conflicts with hand residue and a missing console shard; disclosure risks Nadia and the audit. |
| `threatening_message` | What did “destroy everything you left behind” mean? | The player may mean Voss’s company and reputation, while the detective interprets it as a death threat. |
| `audio_authenticity` | Is the security recording genuine? | The player may deny saying the line at all, then remember using some of its component words in earlier messages. |

## 6. The traps

| Trap | Why it is unseeable | How the detective baits it |
| ---- | ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **The weapon** | The strike occurred in total darkness behind the partition; the counterweight was removed before the player entered. | “When Mara raised the microphone weight, which hand was she using?” The name and weapon are deliberately supplied as if already established. |
| **The killer’s coat** | Red emergency light through wired glass destroys accurate colour perception. | “You described a blue coat in your first account. Was it navy or black?” No colour was ever safely visible. |
| **The victim’s final wound** | Voss was face-down, and the injury was hidden above the player’s viewing angle. | “You saw the fracture before you touched him, correct?” Agreeing fabricates knowledge available only from the medical report. |
| **The words on the master drive** | The player saw only a dark rectangular object and later took the damaged shard, not the labelled master drive. | “Did the drive in her hand read SOVEREIGN AUDIT or CONTINUANCE?” Either choice falsely confirms a readable label. |
| **The full recording** | The player heard one sentence through a distorted monitor, not the longer clip now in evidence. | The detective plays the first half and asks the player to complete it, tempting them to adopt words they never heard. |

## 7. The false positive

The player’s strongest emotional reaction is not to the murder. It is to **Nadia**.

Questions about the access card, synthetic recording and stolen shard cause longer pauses, disrupted cadence and evasive wording because an honest answer could expose a traumatised whistleblower and destroy the only surviving proof that she was framed. VOSS-Continuance correctly detects that these topics matter, but reaches the wrong causal conclusion: it reads protective fear as evidence of a murder conspiracy.

The semantic evidence is equally dangerous. Almost every true fact has a guilty interpretation:

- The player came intending to steal from Voss.
- They hated him and had threatened to destroy his legacy.
- Their voice appears in an incriminating recording.
- They touched the victim and took evidence from the scene.
- They lie about the card and the shard to protect Nadia.
- They know Sovereign can forge audio, which the detective frames as convenient expertise rather than a valid defence.

The dead detective adds a personal bias. It remembers being threatened by the player, but its memory ends before Voss decided to confess. When the player says Voss invited them, the AI experiences this as an attack on its own continuity: accepting the claim would mean accepting that its memories are incomplete and that the man it embodies helped ruin innocent lives.

As the interrogation progresses, the detective builds a written murder theory out of the player’s real statements. It does not invent quotations; it **reframes** them:

> “I came for the audit” becomes “I entered the studio to take Voss’s files.”  
> “I wanted his work destroyed” becomes “I had a declared motive to destroy him.”  
> “I took what was under his hand” becomes “I removed evidence immediately after his death.”

The terrifying result is a case that is factually grounded, emotionally persuasive and completely wrong.

Near the climax, VOSS-Continuance plays the synthetic security clip and asks the player to authenticate their own voice. If challenged with the audit shard, it discovers that the same model family used to reconstruct Voss was used to manufacture Nadia’s confession and the clip implicating the player. The investigator is not merely biased by synthetic evidence—it is itself the final synthetic exhibit created by the company under accusation.

> **The detective can hear the player’s fear. It cannot remember why they are afraid of him.**

## 8. How the endings land

| Ending | Condition | What it sounds like here |
| ------ | --------- | ------------------------ |
| `released_clean` | High consistency, no fabrications; the player reveals the shard and proves the security clip synthetic. | **“The recording is false. Your account is not. Elias Voss brought you here because, at the end, he finally believed Nadia. I did not remember becoming that man.”** |
| `released_suspicious` | Consistent but the player protects Nadia and never gives the detective enough to prove the alternate killer. | **“I cannot place the weapon in your hand. I also cannot explain why my last minutes are missing from yours. You may leave—but whatever you took is still speaking for you.”** |
| `held_for_questioning` | Contradictions remain around the card, timeline or removed evidence. | **“You may be innocent of killing me. Innocence, however, is not the same as an account I can trust. We begin again.”** |
| `charged` | Hard contradictions, a fabricated trap detail or acceptance of the synthetic recording as genuine. | **“I remember that you wanted to destroy me. Now I have your voice explaining how. Case complete.”** |

Optional post-credit sting for any ending in which Sovereign survives:

> A system message appears: **VOICE COVERAGE: 94% — WITNESS MODEL READY.** Then the player hears their own voice ask the next suspect the first question.

## 9. Production cost

- **Interrogation room:** One dark corporate interview room; a monitor or one-way glass displays only a waveform, Voss’s restrained digital portrait and the murder theory assembling from quoted testimony.
- **The three glimpses:** One fixed first-person corridor/studio view, one silhouette, red emergency lighting, a blackout, a fall and a hand reaching toward a blinking console. No facial animation or visible killing blow is required.
- **Audio:** Voss’s main TTS voice; three prerecorded cinematic sounds; one synthetic incriminating line assembled from separately recorded player fragments or a prepared demo-safe fallback. The core case does not depend on real-time voice cloning.
- **Anything unusual this needs that we do not already have:** A simple UI that progressively assembles the detective’s theory from extracted claims; a binary authenticity reveal for the security clip; one scripted transition revealing that the detective is VOSS-Continuance. No additional AI character, explorable environment or procedural mystery generator is needed.

## Core reveal in one paragraph

The player believes they are being questioned about the murder of Dr. Elias Voss, the disgraced founder of a company accused of manufacturing synthetic audio evidence. Midway through the interrogation, they discover the detective is VOSS-Continuance: a posthumous reconstruction trained on the victim’s voice, memories and casework, but cut off before Voss decided to expose his own company. It remembers the player’s hatred but not its cause, and it has been given a synthetic recording that appears to place the player at the murder. To prove their innocence, the player must expose the dead detective’s complicity, reveal that the evidence—and the investigator itself—were manufactured by the same company, and decide whether saving themselves is worth sacrificing the living whistleblower they came to protect.

## Final pitch line

> **You are not trying to convince the police that you did not kill him. You are trying to convince a dead man—rebuilt by the company that forged the evidence against you—that he no longer remembers the truth.**
