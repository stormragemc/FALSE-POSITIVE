# Case idea — *Nobody Went Out*

**By:** Bong · **Date:** 1 Aug 2026

> Five friends, snowed into a cabin, wake to find one of them dead in the cold. Before the road
> even opened, the four survivors agreed on a story — the murderer wrote it — and the player
> signed it. Now he has to explain a memory that keeps contradicting a lie he already told police.

---

## 1. Ground truth — what actually happened

Five friends, the annual winter week at a rented cabin. The road out closes at 23:00; the plough
doesn't reach them until 09:40. Nobody arrived after, nobody left before.

- **~21:00** Heavy drinking. Nico and the player swap coats as a joke — the player ends up in
  Nico's heavy parka, Nico in a thin jacket. Nobody thinks of it again.
- **~23:30** Aaron works out, from something Nico says carelessly, that Nico and **Ivy** — Aaron's
  wife — had been involved for two years, and that everyone else in the group knew. He doesn't
  raise his voice. That's the whole of his reaction.
- **~00:50** The player and Nico argue by the fire — the player telling him to say it out loud
  before Aaron works it out for himself. Nico goes outside to cool off, in a thin jacket, because
  his coat is on the player.
- **~01:14** Outside, cold, Nico opens a voice memo on his phone and starts rehearsing what he's
  going to say to Aaron in the morning. It runs three minutes before the cold kills the battery.
- **~01:10–01:20** The player goes to the door, opens it, calls Nico's name, gets no answer,
  assumes he's round the side, goes back to the sofa, leaves the door **unbolted**, passes out.
  *His own voice is on the last twenty seconds of Nico's memo, calling his name through the door.*
- **~01:40** Aaron crosses the room and throws the bolt, knowing Nico is still outside. Goes to
  bed.
- **~02:00** **Ivy sees him do it**, from the landing. She says nothing then, and says nothing
  now.
- **09:00** Priya finds Nico by the woodshed, twenty metres from the door. Hypothermia.
- **09:20** At the kitchen table, before anyone calls anyone, Aaron proposes the story — to
  protect Ivy, he says. Everyone agrees. Still drunk and half in shock, the player agrees too.
- **09:40** The road opens. Four people give the police the same paragraph:

  > *"We were all asleep by midnight. Nico went out for a smoke. Nobody went out after him.
  > Nobody went to the door. The door was never bolted — he could have come back in any time he
  > wanted."*

  Every clause is false. The last one is Aaron's alibi. The player's name is on all of it.

**Did the player do it? No.** He opened the door, called out, and left it unlocked. What he is
actually guilty of is two years of silence about his best friend's marriage, and forty minutes of
agreeing to a lie at a kitchen table while still drunk, because a man he trusted said it would
protect someone who'd already been hurt enough.

## 2. Why the player is there

- **How they witnessed it:** he didn't, cleanly. Blackout drunk, in and out of consciousness on
  the sofa, in the same room as the door the whole time. What he has are fragments, not a
  sequence — and a signed statement that contradicts every one of them.
- **Why he is a suspect and not just a witness:** he argued with the victim minutes before the
  victim went outside; he was found the next morning wearing the dead man's coat; investigators
  have recovered twenty seconds of the victim's own voice memo with the player's voice on it, at
  the door, at a time his signed statement says he was asleep; and he has no account of himself
  for the eight hours between. The detective is not reaching. Every fact against him is real —
  they just don't mean what they look like.

## 3. The three glimpses

Presented as memory returning to him in the chair — never a cold open — one early, one mid, one
late in the interrogation. Each plays exactly once; ~8 s total. Each fragment demolishes one
clause of the statement he's defending:

| # | When | ~sec | What the player sees / hears | What they cannot tell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | early, ~turn 2 | 3 s | Firelight, too much noise. Nico's face close, saying something urgent. The player's own hand flat on his chest. Cold from somewhere behind. | What Nico said. Whether he's pushing him away or holding him up. Where Aaron and Ivy are. |
| 2 | mid, ~turn 6 | 3 s | The door open on blue-black snow. His own breath. Nico's name in his own mouth. Nothing coming back. His hand on the door edge. | Whether Nico was already out there. Whether he closed it. How long he stood there. |
| 3 | late, ~turn 10 | 2 s | A slice of ceiling from the sofa, much later. A bolt going across. A figure at the door, back turned. | Who it is. Whether he was awake or dreaming it. |

Fragment 1 kills *"asleep by midnight"* — cheap, he can concede it. Fragment 2 kills *"nobody went
to the door"* — expensive, he's now a man who lied to police this morning. Fragment 3 kills *"the
door was never bolted"* — terminal: admitting it protects him and exposes Aaron; defending it
protects Aaron and destroys him.

**What the glimpses deliberately withhold:** who threw the bolt, what Nico said at the fire, the
time of anything, whether the player ever closed that door himself.

## 4. The cast

| Name | Who they are | Where they sit in this |
|---|---|---|
| Nico Vlahos | The dead one. Warm, careless, always talked too much. | Went out in a thin jacket because the player had his coat. His own voice memo is the detective's best lever. |
| Aaron Teague | Married to Ivy. Quiet, controlled, hosts the trip every year. | Threw the bolt. Wrote the statement everyone signed. Author of both crimes. |
| Ivy Teague | Aaron's wife. | Two years with Nico. Saw Aaron at the door from the landing. Cannot speak without becoming the motive — the one person who could clear the player, silenced by the same secret he's been keeping. |
| Priya Raman | Passed out early, woke twice in the night. | Signed the same paragraph. Her account is already starting to wobble in the next room, and the detective mentions it. |

## 5. Topics the detective wants covered

| Topic | The question behind it | Where a contradiction can open |
|---|---|---|
| `the_statement` | Does he still stand by what he signed at 09:20? | First answer: yes, obviously. Falls apart clause by clause as glimpses land. |
| `midnight` | Was everyone really asleep by then? | Fragment 1 lands and moves the timestamp he already committed to |
| `the_argument` | What was it actually about? | Vague or false first pass; costs him the secret once pressed |
| `the_door` | Did he go to it? | Certain he didn't — until fragment 2 |
| `the_memo` | Does he recognise his own voice on Nico's last recording? | Denial first, reversed once played back |
| `the_coat` | Why was he wearing Nico's parka? | Minimised as nothing, then the timeline makes it matter |
| `the_bolt` | Was the door ever locked? | Contradicts his own statement the moment fragment 3 lands |
| `who_wrote_it` | Whose idea was the story at the kitchen table? | The question that ends Aaron, if the player will ever say it |
| `who_knew` | Who in the group knew about Ivy and Nico? | Forces him to admit his own two years of complicit silence |
| `the_morning` | What did he do when Priya started screaming? | Small details drift between tellings |

## 6. The traps

| Trap | Why it is unseeable | How the detective baits it |
|---|---|---|
| Whether Nico was already outside at the door | Dark, snow, no answer to his call | "Did you hear him out there, or just assume?" |
| Who was at the door in fragment 3 | Back turned, seen through half-consciousness | "Which of them was it?" — naming Aaron is a fabrication that happens to be correct; the detective catches the fabrication and the player is punished for being right |
| The time of anything that night | No clock appears in any fragment | "You're very precise about 1:10. How do you know?" |
| Bolt thrown before or after he lay down | No continuous consciousness to order the two events by | "So the door was bolted before you fell asleep, or after?" |
| Nico's condition at the door | Never saw him again after the fire | Claiming Nico answered back contradicts both the memo and the pathologist's estimate |

## 7. The false positive

The detective is right about almost everything it detects. The account *is* rehearsed — four
people gave the same paragraph. The stress spikes on the door and the bolt *are* real. The man
*is* concealing a crime. Every inference the system makes is sound, and the conclusion is wrong,
because the crime he's actually hiding was committed at a kitchen table at twenty past nine in
the morning by someone else — and he agreed to it out of decency, not guilt.

Underneath that: he was blackout drunk and **genuinely cannot rule himself out.** He isn't
defending his innocence in that chair. He's guessing at it, out loud, in front of something that
can hear him guess — and every hedge, whether it comes from shame, loyalty, or a hole in his own
memory, sounds exactly the same coming out of his mouth.

## 8. How the endings land

| Ending | Condition | What it sounds like here |
|---|---|---|
| `released_clean` | High consistency, no fabrications | "You're the only one of the four who stopped saying the paragraph. That took you nine minutes. It'll take the others longer." |
| `released_suspicious` | Consistent but the detective never got enough | "Consistent. That isn't the same as complete. Stay where we can reach you." |
| `held_for_questioning` | Contradictions unresolved | "You've given me two different one o'clocks, and a door that was open and bolted at the same time. Sit with that." |
| `charged` | Hard contradictions or a caught fabrication | "You signed a page this morning that says nobody went to that door. Then you told me you did. I didn't write either of those. You did, both times." |

The outcome screen quotes the player back to themselves, then gives one detail they never had:
*"14:20 — Ivy Teague has asked to make a second statement. She is still waiting."*

## 9. Production cost

- **Interrogation room:** the shared build, unchanged.
- **The three glimpses:** the cheapest set in the whole idea pool. One cabin interior —
  firelight, a sofa, a door. The only exterior is a doorway of blue-black snow: a flat plate and
  a particle pass, no landscape. Fragment 3 is a ceiling and a sound.
- **Audio:** fire crackle, wind, a bolt sliding across, muffled voices, one door. Library/synth
  throughout. No VO but the detective — the memo is *read aloud by the detective*, not played as
  a second voice, keeping to the "nobody but the detective has a voice" rule; if the team ever
  wants to spend one 20-second take on Nico's actual voice, that's the single highest-value line
  of audio in the build, but the case works without it.
- **Anything unusual this needs that we do not already have:** distributing the fragments across
  the interrogation (instead of a single cold open) needs a new server→client event to fire a
  fragment mid-session, and a client-side pause on push-to-talk while it plays — a small addition
  to the frozen §3.4 protocol and a two-line change to §4.1 of the implementation plan. This is
  the one condition of the idea, and it's Ado's and Giorgi's call, not mine. Every glimpse still
  plays exactly once, so it doesn't touch the "no replay" rule — only *when* they play. If the
  team doesn't want the contract change, the case still works with all three glimpses in a cold
  open; it loses the beat where a fragment contradicts something the player already swore to two
  turns earlier.
