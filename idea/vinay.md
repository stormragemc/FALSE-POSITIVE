# Case idea — *The Amber Light*

**By:** Vinay · **Date:** 1 Aug 2026

> After a humiliating graduate interview at an electric-vehicle factory, the player returns for
> a forgotten phone and witnesses a battery-lab explosion. By morning, their desperation, angry
> words, and visitor-badge record have turned the only nearby outsider into the prime suspect.

---

## 1. Ground truth — what actually happened

The player attended a graduate engineering assessment at a fictional electric-vehicle factory
with two friends. They understood the technical material but froze under pressure, gave halting
answers, and contradicted themselves while explaining their reasoning. The interviewer, Dr. Elian
Voss, entered a blunt assessment into the internal recruitment system: technically promising, but
not suitable for safety-critical work.

Embarrassed, the player told their friends that the interview had gone reasonably well. While
venting about Voss, they said, “I’d do anything to make him take that assessment back.” One friend
had performed exceptionally and had been told to expect an offer, making the player's failure feel
worse.

After the group left, the player realised that their phone was still in the interview room. They
returned alone with their temporary visitor badge while one friend waited outside. On the way to
the interview room, the player passed the reinforced window of the adjacent battery-testing lab.

Inside the lab, senior battery engineer Sera Vale was running a cell test under deadline pressure.
She had placed the test rig into maintenance mode and bypassed its automatic thermal cutoff. Dr.
Voss was beside the rig when the cell entered thermal runaway. Vale moved away from the control
station moments before the cell exploded. Voss was critically injured; the player survived because
the corridor fire shutter dropped between them and the lab.

During the evacuation, Vale deleted the local maintenance-override entry from the surviving
control tablet. She later told investigators that the automatic safeguards had been functioning.
The ruptured cell casing therefore appeared, in the preliminary examination, as though somebody
had physically damaged it. Vale did not plant evidence or name the player; she concealed her own
negligence and allowed the circumstantial evidence to point elsewhere.

The player **did not cause the explosion**. The real offences are Vale's criminal negligence,
tampering with the safety record, and subsequent obstruction.

## 2. Why the player is there

- **How they witnessed it:** The player returned alone for a phone left in the interview room. The
  route took them along the corridor outside the battery lab, where a narrow reinforced window,
  reflections, and the descending fire shutter gave them only fragmentary views.
- **Why they are a suspect and not just a witness:** Their badge logged them re-entering the wing
  shortly before the blast. Damaged footage places them near the lab but does not show them
  retrieving the phone. Investigators also have Voss's harsh interview assessment, the player's
  technically capable background, their angry remark, and a preliminary theory that the cell was
  deliberately punctured. One friend can confirm the phone story only partially and estimates that
  the player was inside much longer than the player remembers.

Before the cinematic, the player receives this short role card so they are never punished for
canonical backstory they were not told:

> You came to the factory with two friends for a graduate engineering assessment. You froze during
> the interview but told them it went fine. After leaving, you returned alone for your forgotten
> phone.

## 3. The three glimpses

This is the entire crime cinematic: three fragments, approximately eight seconds total, never
replayed.

| # | ~sec | What the player sees / hears | What they cannot tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 s | Passing the reinforced window, the player sees Voss beside the battery rig and Vale near the control station. An amber warning is flashing. | The warning's label, the console readout, what either person is doing with the rig, or whether any safeguard has been disabled. |
| 2 | 3 s | While reaching the interview room, the player catches a warped reflection of a pale protective figure moving away from the lab. A heavy door closes somewhere behind them. | The figure's face, sex, badge, exact clothing colour, direction after leaving the reflection, or even whether it is Vale. |
| 3 | 2 s | A sharp alarm tone sounds. A violent flash fills the lab window, Voss's silhouette disappears, the blast strikes, and the corridor fire shutter drops. | What initiated the blast, whether the cell casing was already damaged, where Vale is, or what happens inside after the shutter blocks the view. |

**What the glimpses deliberately withhold:** The safety setting, the contents of the test console,
the identity of the reflected figure, the physical condition of the cell before the blast, and
every spoken exchange inside the sound-insulated lab.

## 4. The cast

All names, people, and organisations in this case are fictional.

| Name | Who they are | Where they sit in this |
|---|---|---|
| Dr. Elian Voss | The blunt engineering interviewer and test director | Wrote the damaging assessment and was critically injured beside the rig, making him the apparent target of the player's resentment. |
| Sera Vale | The factory's senior battery engineer | Bypassed the thermal cutoff, survived the blast, removed the override record, and concealed her negligence without directly framing the player. |
| Kian Rook | The player's close friend and fellow applicant | Performed well, received an encouraging offer signal, and later reports the player's angry remark accurately but without its emotional context. |
| Mira Sen | The second friend and fellow applicant | Waited outside when the player returned for the phone. She supports the reason for returning but cannot verify the route and overestimates how long the player was gone. |

## 5. Topics the detective wants covered

| Topic | The question behind it | Where a contradiction can open |
|---|---|---|
| `interview_performance` | How badly did the assessment go, and what did Voss say? | “Reasonably well” versus freezing, missing answers, or being told the role required stronger composure. |
| `friends_and_remark` | What did the player tell the group, how did Kian's success affect them, and what did “anything” mean? | Pride versus jealousy; admitting the failure now versus claiming it went well; a plea for another chance versus retaliation. |
| `forgotten_phone` | Where was the phone, when did the player notice, and why return alone? | Whether they noticed at the door, gate, or car; whether they asked a friend or guard to come. |
| `return_timing` | When did the player re-enter and how long were they inside? | The player's stressed estimate of one or two minutes versus Mira's estimate of roughly five. |
| `route_and_access` | Which doors and corridors did the player use? | Whether they passed the lab once or twice, tried any lab door, or deviated from the interview-room route. |
| `people_in_the_lab` | Where were Voss and Vale during the first glimpse? | At the rig versus at the console; standing still versus moving; what the player saw versus later inferred. |
| `amber_warning` | What exactly did the flashing warning show? | The player saw its colour but could not read its label or know what system produced it. |
| `reflected_figure` | Who moved away from the lab, and what did they look like? | Observation versus assumption: identity, build, badge, clothing colour, and direction are all uncertain. |
| `blast_sequence` | What was the order of the door sound, alarm, flash, blast, and shutter? | A chaotic two-second sequence invites transposed sounds and changing claims about what came first. |
| `after_the_blast` | What did the player do after the shutter fell? | Whether they called for help, tried the shutter, retrieved the phone first, or immediately evacuated. |

## 6. The traps

| Trap | Why it is unseeable | How the detective baits it |
|---|---|---|
| The amber warning's text | The indicator was distant, glimpsed for seconds, and viewed through reinforced glass. | “You said the thermal warning was already active. What exactly did it say?” |
| The reflected figure's identity | The player saw only a distorted reflection with no face, readable badge, or reliable colour. | “When Vale came out of the lab, which door did she take?” |
| The alleged puncture | The cell casing was inside the rig and never visible from the corridor, before or after the blast. | “How large was the hole you saw in the battery casing?” |
| The cutoff state | The control display faced away and was too far away to read. | “Was the automatic cutoff shown as armed or disabled?” |
| A conversation inside the lab | The reinforced lab was sound-insulated; the player heard no dialogue through it. | “What were Voss and Vale arguing about when you passed them?” |
| Vale's badge or protective gear | Neither was directly visible in the warped reflection. | “You recognised her department stripe on the protective coat, didn't you?” |

## 7. The false positive

The player has the same weakness in both rooms: they know the material, but pressure makes their
speech halting and inconsistent. Voss misread that behaviour as professional incompetence. The
detective can now misread it as guilt.

The player is ashamed of failing, ashamed of lying to their friends about it, and uncomfortable
admitting jealousy toward Kian. Their angry remark sounds much worse after the explosion than it
did when spoken. Their reason for returning is real, but only partly corroborated. Stress compresses
their sense of time, while Mira's casual estimate expands it. Finally, the player may honestly
remember the reflected figure yet become overconfident about details inferred after hearing the
detective's theory.

An innocent player therefore has several reasons to hesitate, correct themselves, minimise an
emotion, or protect their dignity. None requires them to have caused the explosion, but every one
can look evasive from the other side of the table.

## 8. How the endings land

These are immediate procedural outcomes the morning after the explosion, not final verdicts. The
real negligence is only hinted at, never fully explained during the interrogation.

| Ending | Condition | What it sounds like here |
|---|---|---|
| `released_clean` | High consistency, no fabrications | “You're free to go. The lab's record says there was no amber warning. You remembered one.” |
| `released_suspicious` | Consistent but the detective never got enough | “I cannot hold you on what I have. Do not mistake that for me believing you.” |
| `held_for_questioning` | Contradictions unresolved | “Your story keeps changing in the minutes before that fire. You're staying here while we check it again.” |
| `charged` | Hard contradictions or a caught fabrication | “You described a puncture you could never have seen. You're being charged in connection with the explosion.” |

## 9. Production cost

- **Interrogation room:** The existing single first-person police interview room; no case-specific
  geometry is required beyond evidence photographs or notebook text already supported by the UI.
- **The three glimpses:** One factory corridor, one reinforced lab window, a mostly static test-rig
  background, two silhouettes, an amber emissive light, a reflection overlay, a flash, smoke, camera
  shake, and a descending fire shutter. Stills with small animated elements are sufficient.
- **Audio:** Factory ambience, one heavy door, a short alarm tone, a contained explosion, debris,
  the shutter impact, and muffled evacuation noise. Nobody in the case needs a voiced line.
- **Anything unusual this needs that we do not already have:** A convincing warped-window
  reflection is the only unusual visual. It can be baked into the second still rather than rendered
  dynamically. No vehicle, crowd, fire simulation, gore, or destroyed-factory exterior is needed.
